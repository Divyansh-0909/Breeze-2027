# Build a living gully from layered, budgeted sound

Open-world games don't play a single ambience track. They build each place from a few quiet, anonymous looping beds plus a large number of positioned, randomized detail sounds. Each sound gets its own curves for volume, low-pass filtering, spread and reverb send over distance. A mixer then ducks, switches between states and avoids repetition. **GTA V ran this on 949 ambient zones and 1,210 spawn rules, up from 86 zones and 57 rules in GTA IV** ([GDC 2014 slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/GDC2014/Presentations/MacGregor_Alastair_The_Sound_of.pdf)). Naughty Dog built each area of The Last of Us Part II from "hundreds of overlapping emitters" ([A Sound Effect](https://www.asoundeffect.com/the-last-of-us-part-ii-sound/)). Every part of that pipeline maps onto built-in Web Audio nodes. PannerNode handles attenuation, BiquadFilterNode handles air absorption and occlusion, a shared ConvolverNode handles reverb zones, GainNode buses handle mix states, and `AudioBufferSourceNode.start(when)` gives sample-accurate footsteps. The recommended plan for the night gully has these parts:

- 2 to 3 recorded beds.
- 15 to 30 positioned stall emitters.
- A rule-driven scatter of one-shots.
- Two shared convolvers, one for the lane and one for the plaza. A single "plaza openness" value, driven by the player's position, blends between them and also reveals the stage screen.
- Footsteps triggered by distance walked, using 4 to 8 jittered variants per surface and locked to the head-bob cycle.

The budget is about 24 to 32 voices, HRTF on only a handful of emitters, two convolvers, filter updates at 10 to 20 Hz, and Opus-encoded assets. One caveat applies throughout: public AAA sources describe architecture far more often than numbers. Many parameter values below are design starting points to tune by ear. They are not shipped settings.

## Hundreds of randomized emitters, not louder loops, make a street feel alive

The pipelines that are documented all split ambience the same way. Continuous **beds are "a variety of stereo loops like room tones, wind, the hum of a city, distant traffic"**. Any recognizable detail is cut out of them, because it "will become familiar and recognisable to the player as the ambience bed repeats". That detail comes back as separate spot effects whose timing can vary ([Game Audio Learning Portal](https://www.gameaudiolearning.com/knowledgebase/how-to-make-ambiences-for-games)). The spot layer is where the size of a game's world shows. In GTA V, rules outnumber static emitters by more than two to one (**1,210 rules against 518 static emitters**) ([GDC 2014 slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/GDC2014/Presentations/MacGregor_Alastair_The_Sound_of.pdf)). Most of its density therefore comes from rule-driven, randomly spawned sounds rather than hand placement.

Sucker Punch moved the same way on Ghost of Tsushima: "In the past we hand placed all of our emitters in the world, and with a game the size of Ghost, that would not be possible." A data-driven system decided which animal species played, when and how often. Hand-placed exceptions were kept for special spots, such as trees painted with rustling-leaf loops on Iki Island ([A Sound Effect](https://www.asoundeffect.com/ghost-of-tsushima-sound/)). Its sequel replaced static ambience loops with **four mono emitters around the player** that blend wind and environment sounds by biome ([A Sound Effect](https://www.asoundeffect.com/ghost-of-yotei-game-audio/)). The basic scatter logic is simple:

1. Wait a random interval.
2. Pick a sound group and a variation.
3. Place the sound at a random offset from the listener.

This is how the Eon Altar devlog describes it ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/video-game-audio-under-the-hood-eon-altar-ambience-system)). Middleware adds probability, randomized start offsets and randomized delays on top ([Berklee Online](https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/how-to-make-your-interactive-game-soundscapes-come-alive/)).

Two further techniques fit the gully directly. In **The Witcher 3, zone emitters follow the listener while inside a designer-placed volume and stay clamped to its boundary once the player leaves**. Local ambiences override the global weather layer through a volume-linked parameter ([Audiokinetic Wwise Tour 2016](https://www.audiokinetic.com/en/learn/videos/vjuui_dw8cc/?course=wwise251&lesson=1)). Crowd sound there was split into four quadrants around the listener, driven by how close and how many NPCs were nearby ([Audiokinetic](https://www.audiokinetic.com/en/learning/videos/bv4lbbrmu0a/)). Long features use splines instead of single points. Tsushima's guiding wind travelled on **three splines spaced about 5 m apart** ([A Sound Effect](https://www.asoundeffect.com/ghost-of-tsushima-sound/)), and a river can be "a spline through the middle of the river" with one emitter attached ([Game Audio Learning Portal](https://www.gameaudiolearning.com/knowledgebase/how-to-make-ambiences-for-games)). In the browser, the spline emitter becomes one PannerNode moved each frame to the point on a line closest to the listener. That suits the plaza edge or a road crossing the far end of the lane. The Witcher clamp becomes a plaza-crowd emitter that sits at the plaza mouth while the player is in the lane and follows the player once they are inside.

The transition from lane to plaza is where a cheap implementation most often gives itself away. A review of Watch Dogs 2 praised its city ambience but singled out zone changes at doorways that felt like **a simple timed crossfade** ([The Sound Architect](https://thesoundarchitect.co.uk/?p=16397)). The fix is a continuous value, `p` from 0 to 1, computed from the player's distance to the plaza mouth over roughly 8 to 15 m (a starting value, not a sourced one). This single value moves several things at once:

- The beds (lane room tone down, plaza crowd and open-air wash up).
- The reverb blend between the two convolvers.
- The stage screen's filter.

Because `p` follows the player's position, the change tracks the player's steps and never runs on its own clock.

Night is also handled with parameters. Red Dead Redemption tuned bugs, birds and animals to weather and time of day ([Designing Sound](https://designingsound.org/2010/08/20/red-dead-redemption-exclusive-interview-with-audio-director-jeffrey-whitcher/)). Marvel's Spider-Man built separate day, dusk and night sound palettes ([A Sound Effect](https://www.asoundeffect.com/marvels-spider-man-sound/)). Sleeping Dogs varied ambience by location, time and weather ([Audiokinetic](https://www.audiokinetic.com/ko/blog/interview-with-sleeping-dogs)). For a late-evening market, that suggests a quieter traffic bed and more isolated one-shots with longer gaps. A quieter bed lets distant detail through, such as a dog, a far horn or a late scooter.

No game postmortem covers Indian street ambience, so the palette comes from field-recording and cultural sources. Hildegard Westerkamp describes Delhi traffic horns as signals (bicycle bells, scooter horns, bus horns) and notes that "every neighbourhood has its own signals and soundmarks, where temple bells or the call of the muezzin signal times of worship" ([Westerkamp](https://www.hildegardwesterkamp.ca/writings/writings-by/?post_id=22&title=soundscapes-of-cities)). The azaan reaches a city through loudspeakers ([Maitra, SAGE 2023](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/23938617221109932)). These religious soundmarks carry real local meaning and fixed times, so they should be placed deliberately and timed with care. They should not be random scatter elements. Recorded material exists. One example is a commercial Ahmedabad library that includes the Manek Chowk night food market and temple aartis (**Rs 4,999**) ([Treehouse Samples](https://www.treehousesamples.com/cityinsoundamd)). Another is a free walking recording of a Mumbai vegetable market ([Freesound](https://freesound.org/people/The_Sound_Side/sounds/276590/)). Freesound licences are set per file: CC0 is free to use, CC-BY needs credit, and non-commercial licences rule out monetized use ([gtstu guide](https://gtstu.com/?p=5011)).

| Layer | Gully content (suggested) | Web Audio implementation | Count |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beds | Distant city/traffic wash, lane room tone, plaza crowd murmur | Recorded stereo loops of 30 to 120 s, non-positional, streamed, crossfaded by `p` | 2 to 3 |
| Positioned loops | Tawa/kadhai sizzle, generator or inverter hum, stall fan, shop radio playing a film song | Mono source, then lowpass, then PannerNode, plus a reverb send | 15 to 30, culled by distance |
| Scatter one-shots | Scooter pass-by and horn tap, bicycle bell, dog bark, shutter rattle, ladle clang, vendor call | Random interval, variant and position, with per-category cooldowns | Up to 4 to 6 live at once |
| Line / zone emitters | Plaza crowd edge, cross-street traffic | One panner kept at the closest point on a segment, or clamped to a zone boundary | 1 to 2 |
| Deliberate soundmarks | Temple bell or aarti, distant train horn | Hand-placed and time-gated, never random | 1 to 3 |

Recordings are required for the beds. Wind, rain and water "can be synthesized to a high standard" ([Designing Sound](https://designingsound.org/2012/06/13/audiogaming-an-interview/)), and FXive built rain and wind models entirely on Web Audio ([Reiss et al., IBC 2018](https://joshreiss.github.io/documents/2018/Reiss%20-%20FXive%20-%20IBC.pdf)). Crowds, traffic and footsteps are different. Footsteps have many control parameters, and granular methods handle contact sounds poorly ([procedural audio slides](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1FwQPPxXfKgMNSCOQ2_hqPytZuup8_2-Ro0VS4TRglaE/htmlpresent)). A crowd bed made from filtered noise sounds like static or hiss. The cure is not to make it louder. Keep the bed low and anonymous, use real recordings, and move the sense of identity into the spot sounds.

## Distance is four curves, and the reverb path carries the corner

Engines treat distance as several separate curves per sound, not one volume knob. Wwise attenuation curves control volume, low-pass filtering and spread. One tutorial's starting shape keeps full volume from 0 to 5 m, fades it from 5 to 50 m and cuts it off beyond 50 m ([Generalist Programmer](https://generalistprogrammer.com/tutorials/wwise-complete-game-audio-middleware-tutorial)). Unreal simulates air absorption with a low-pass filter "where the cutoff frequency is lowered as the sound source is further away", and also offers per-distance reverb send methods ([Unreal docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/sound-attenuation-in-unreal-engine)). Wwise 2023.1 applies distance attenuation to the wet path so that each sound can have "a unique 'wet' attenuation curve". It also warns that a room reverb bus needs a flattened curve, or attenuation gets applied twice ([Audiokinetic](https://www.audiokinetic.com/en/community/blog/revised-aux-send-model/)). Meta's spatializer exposes "Reverb Reach", which raises reverb linearly with distance "to counteract direct sound attenuation" ([Meta](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/unity/meta-xr-audio-sdk-wwise-parameter-reference/)). The pattern is consistent across engines: as a source recedes, it gets quieter, duller, more point-like and relatively wetter.

PannerNode covers only the first of these curves. It offers `linear`, `inverse` (the default) and `exponential` distance models. The inverse model computes gain as `refDistance / (refDistance + rolloffFactor * (max(distance, refDistance) - refDistance))` ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/PannerNode/distanceModel)). With `refDistance` at 1 to 3 m and `rolloffFactor` at 1, that gives a physically plausible drop of about 6 dB per doubling of distance for point sources such as fans and radios. **The inverse and exponential models never reach silence. `maxDistance` only clamps the linear model** ([W3C spec](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio/); this is a recalled spec detail worth checking). Distant emitters therefore keep using CPU unless code stops them. That makes it necessary to add an explicit cull distance of about 40 to 50 m, or to use `linear` for sources that must fade out completely.

The browser has no built-in model of air absorption. The standard pattern is a BiquadFilter low-pass on each emitter, with its cutoff driven by camera distance, as in the three.js sound sandbox's `PositionalAudioFilter` ([munshkr/threejs-3d-sound-sandbox](https://github.com/munshkr/threejs-3d-sound-sandbox); [O'Reilly](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/web-audio-api/9781449332679/ch06.html)). Over a 50 m lane, true air absorption is slight. Most of the sense that a sound is "far and around the corner" comes from occlusion and from a lower direct-to-reverb ratio, not from air.

Occlusion is where the most useful idea in AAA practice comes from. Naughty Dog's approach on The Last of Us was to leave "the direct path audio alone and [fudge] the reflected path - a really wide-band filter on the reflected path so it doesn't roll off" ([Audio Gang PDF](https://www.audiogang.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/GANG-IESD-BEST-MIX-2013-INTERVIEW-NAUGHTY-DOG-THE-LAST-OF-US.pdf)). CryEngine accumulates a `sound_obstruction` value from every surface a ray crosses. It uses a centre ray plus outer rays to tell obstruction (direct path blocked) apart from occlusion (all paths blocked) ([CryEngine docs](https://www.cryengine.com/docs/static/engines/cryengine-5/categories/23756816/pages/44964914)). GTA V used portal-based interior occlusion ([community documentation](https://github.com/nikez/gtav_audio_occlusion_documentation)). In the gully, the browser version works like this:

- Run a `THREE.Raycaster` from the listener to each emitter at 5 to 10 Hz, staggered across frames, against a simplified collision mesh. Authored "same lane / around the corner" flags work too.
- Map a blocked result to a cutoff of about 600 to 1,200 Hz and a gain drop of 6 to 12 dB.
- **Take the reverb send before the occlusion filter.** An occluded stall radio stays dull and quiet directly but still fills the lane's reverb, so it seems to come around the corner.

These mapping values are design starting points, not sourced ones.

Reverb zones follow physics the browser can approximate cheaply. GTA V shipped occlusion, reflections and reverberation as separate systems ([GDC 2014 slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/GDC2014/Presentations/MacGregor_Alastair_The_Sound_of.pdf)). Cyberpunk 2077 reused its reflection system for in-world radios, TVs and clubs ([A Sound Effect](https://www.asoundeffect.com/cyberpunk-2077-sound/)). Sound travels at about 343 m/s. A wall 3 m away therefore returns an echo after about 17 ms, and a wall 6 m away after about 35 ms. A 4 to 6 m wide gully produces a tight, bright flutter of reflections between 12 and 35 ms. Plaza facades 20 to 40 m away produce sparse, distinct echoes at about 120 to 230 ms over a much drier direct sound. That difference should drive the setup:

- **Lane:** a short synthetic convolution IR (RT60 about 0.6 to 1.2 s) plus two DelayNodes tuned to the left and right wall distances. Keep feedback below 0.3 and put a low-pass in the loop.
- **Plaza:** a quieter, darker IR (RT60 about 1.5 to 2.5 s).

These RT60 values are starting points to tune. The spec puts no cap on IR length: a request for one was closed WONTFIX even though "a huge buffer could easily destroy performance" ([W3C ISSUE-73](https://www.w3.org/2011/audio/track/issues/73)). Most of a reverb's character sits in the early part of the IR, so truncating to about 1 s changes the sound little while cutting CPU substantially ([pipedal discussion](https://github.com/rerdavies/pipedal/discussions/98)). Use two shared convolvers whose wet gains are crossfaded by `p`. Don't swap `buffer` on a convolver that is playing. Routing all in-world sounds through the shared reverb is what makes them sound as if they share the player's space ([Splice](https://splice.com/blog/dynamic-game-audio-mix/)).

The stage screen should be "worldized", meaning treated as a PA system inside the scene rather than as a soundtrack. "High-pass filtering and reverberation are applied to musical samples to give the impression of a diegetic source" ([Sonic Field](https://sonicfield.org/wiki/trans-diegetic)). Small speakers can use CRI's radio recipe: peaking bands around 1 and 2 kHz, shelves around 700 Hz and 4 kHz, then distortion ([CRI blog](https://blog.criware.com/index.php/2022/07/07/radio-voice-effect/)). For the stage, use two positioned emitters for left and right PA stacks with a gentle high-pass around 80 to 120 Hz. Add optional cones (inner 60 to 90°, outer 180 to 270°, outer gain 0.2 to 0.4; these are design values). From inside the lane, the walls occlude it and distance dulls it, so the player hears mostly bass and reverberant wash. Clarity and direction arrive only when the player turns into the plaza. The "reveal" is the same chain of distance, occlusion and wet-ratio settings, not a scripted event.

| Distance cue | What AAA engines expose | Web Audio equivalent | Starting mapping (tune by ear) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Volume roll-off | Per-sound volume curve, cut-off distance | `PannerNode` distance model | `inverse`, ref 1 to 3 m, rolloff 1, cull at 40 to 50 m |
| Air absorption | Low-pass cutoff with distance | `BiquadFilterNode` lowpass | About 20 kHz near, falling to a few kHz at 40 to 50 m |
| Wet/dry | Wet attenuation curve, Reverb Reach | Send `GainNode` into a shared `ConvolverNode` | Direct falls faster than the send |
| Occlusion | Ray or portal obstruction values | Raycast driving lowpass and gain | 600 to 1,200 Hz, -6 to -12 dB, send kept open |
| Space | Reverb zones, reflection systems | Two convolvers plus wall DelayNodes, crossfaded by `p` | Lane 12 to 35 ms reflections, plaza 120 to 230 ms echoes |

## Footsteps and head bob should follow the stride, not a timer

The footstep pipeline is the same across Unreal, Unity, Wwise and FMOD:

1. Detect the surface under the foot. In Unreal this is the hit result's physical material ([Unreal forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/different-footstep-sounds-on-different-materials/3170)).
2. Choose that surface's pool of samples. Wwise does this with a Switch Container holding one Random Container per surface ([Audiokinetic](https://www.audiokinetic.com/en/footsteps-material-management-using-wwise-unreal-engine-4-unity-3d)).
3. Pick a variant without immediate repeats.
4. Apply small amounts of random variation in pitch and volume (jitter).

The usual guidance is **4 to 8 variations per surface, with pitch jitter of a few percent**. Three surface groups (hard, soft, metal) "deliver most of the effect" ([Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/footstep-sounds-matter-more-than-you-think)). One worked Unreal project used ±5% pitch and ±3 dB volume ([L-Pace](https://l-pace.github.io/projects/audio-programming-unreal)). Heel, scrape and debris layers that fire only some of the time break up patterns ([Unreal forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/fps-footstep-sounds/2697565)). Middleware separates sprinting from walking with a movement-speed parameter ([Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/footstep-sounds-matter-more-than-you-think)). In the browser, that means a separate, heavier and brighter run sample set, not walk samples played faster. Naughty Dog tied The Last of Us Part II's Foley to its motion-matching animation system ([A Sound Effect](https://asoundeffect.com/the-last-of-us-part-ii-sound/)). The broader lesson is that movement sound should come from the movement model itself.

The step rate should come from real gait, because game speeds are deliberately unrealistic. A gait study measured cadence of **95.7 steps/min at a slow walk, 111.9 at preferred speed and 126.5 at a fast walk**, with step lengths of 0.59, 0.71 and 0.84 m ([J NeuroEng Rehabil 2009](https://jneuroengrehab.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1743-0003-6-9/tables/1)). Those figures work out to about 0.94, 1.32 and 1.77 m/s. Cadence tracks speed closely (r = 0.97), and 130 steps/min marks vigorous walking ([Tudor-Locke et al., BJSM 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29858465/)). Walking turns into running near 2.0 m/s ([Fiveable](https://fiveable.me/motor-learning-control/unit-15/biomechanics-walking-running/study-guide/8brWjHcUozbsMlK6)). Games run much faster: Unreal's default MaxWalkSpeed is 6 m/s ([Unreal forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/what-units-are-used-for-movement-speed-in-ue4/282127)). A playtest thesis found that a slower character speed encourages deeper exploration ([Drexel](https://researchdiscovery.drexel.edu/esploro/outputs/graduate/991022059038804721)). That supports a deliberately slow walk for a market lane.

Trigger steps by **distance walked, not by a fixed timer**. Accumulate the distance the player travels and plant a foot each time it passes the stride length. Cadence then follows speed automatically, including during the ease between walk and sprint. Schedule each step on the audio clock: when the next foot plant falls within a 50 to 100 ms look-ahead, call `src.start(ctx.currentTime + dt)`. This is the "Tale of Two Clocks" pattern ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/audio-scheduling)). It gives sample-accurate onsets without an AudioWorklet. The player's own footsteps should play unpanned and stay clearly above the beds. One Apex Legends player complained that self-footsteps were "so quiet in the mix" that players lose feedback ([EA forums](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/apex-legends-feedback-en/re-your-footsteps-in-the-audio-mix/8425952)).

Head bob is both a feedback channel and a comfort risk. Xbox Accessibility Guideline 117 names walking-bob and head-bob as motion-sickness triggers and asks games to avoid them or to provide options to turn them off. It cites Elder Scrolls Online's adjustable bob intensity as an example ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117)). Some players feel sick even with minimal bob, so reducing it is not enough without an off switch ([Madeline Miller](https://madelinemiller.dev/blog/motion-sickness-accessibility/)). Chen et al. found that **raising the velocity or frequency of a visual oscillation worsened sickness, while changing amplitude at constant velocity did not** ([Ergonomics 2016](https://www.ieda.ust.hk/dfaculty/so/pdf/Ergo-Chen-et-al-2016-vol59-no4.pdf)). That rules out the common habit of scaling both bob amplitude and frequency up for sprint. Since frequency must rise with cadence, sprint amplitude should stay close to the walk amplitude. Lock vertical bob to the step rate and lateral sway to half of it. Fire the footstep at the bottom of each bob cycle so sight and sound agree. Many games "subtly increase the FOV when the player sprints" ([GDQuest](https://www.gdquest.com/library/glossary/field_of_view)). If used, the FOV kick belongs under the same motion setting.

| Parameter | Slow walk | Shift sprint | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Speed | 1.0 to 1.3 m/s | 3.5 to 4 m/s | Gait data for walk; sprint is a design choice above the 2.0 m/s run transition |
| Stride (distance per step) | 0.6 to 0.7 m | 1.2 to 1.4 m | Gait step lengths; longer sprint stride is inferred |
| Step interval | About 0.55 to 0.6 s | About 0.35 s | Speed divided by stride |
| Footstep set | Walk pool, 4 to 8 per surface | Run pool, heavier and brighter, plus an optional scuff layer | Tutorial and forum consensus |
| Jitter | ±3 to 5% rate, ±2 to 3 dB, ±10 to 20 ms | Same | Forum and tutorial values; timing jitter is inferred |
| Vertical bob | About 2 cm at step rate | About 2.5 to 3 cm at step rate | Unsourced; kept low per Chen et al. |
| Lateral sway | About 1 to 2 cm at half the step rate | Similar | Unsourced |
| Transition | Ease speed, bob and FOV over 0.2 to 0.4 s; FOV kick about +5° or none | Same | Unsourced; toggle required per XAG 117 |

No source gave studio figures for sprint breathing, clothing rustle, the level difference between first-person and third-person footsteps, or engine defaults for bob amplitude. Treat those layers as optional and tune them by feel.

## A small mixer and shuffle bags stop fatigue and repetition

AAA dynamic mixing scales sounds against each other rather than squashing them with compression. DICE's HDR audio for Battlefield: Bad Company measures loudness at the listener and scales sources against the loudest audible ones, using authored loudness values. "HDR is not compression"; the waveform is never touched ([EA Frostbite](https://ea.com/frostbite/news/how-hdr-audio-makes-battlefield-bad-company-go-boom)). Wwise implements the same idea as a moving "window": louder sounds push the window up and quieter sounds drop out ([Designing Sound](https://designingsound.org/2013/06/21/finding-your-way-with-high-dynamic-range-audio-in-wwise/)). Mix states are switched with FMOD snapshots, "an instanceable unit of changes to the project mix" ([FMOD docs](https://www.fmod.com/docs/2.03/studio/mixing.html)). One worked example attenuates exterior ambience by **-6 dB with a low-pass when the player steps indoors** ([L-Pace](https://l-pace.github.io/projects/audio-programming-unreal)). Priority ducking can be built from nested groups with a compressor that lowers low-priority layers, as Roblox's Duvall Drive did ([Roblox Docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/resources/the-mystery-of-duvall-drive/design-dark-soundscapes)).

The browser version is a short bus graph. Category GainNodes (beds, spot ambience, scatter, stage, footsteps) feed a master `DynamicsCompressorNode` set up as a limiter. A lightweight form of HDR comes from ducking the beds 2 to 4 dB when a loud one-shot plays, such as a scooter horn or a shutter slam. Use roughly 50 ms attack and 0.5 to 1 s release (unsourced values). Useful mix states include:

- **Sprint:** beds a couple of dB down, footsteps up.
- **Idle:** beds restored and a slightly higher scatter rate, so standing still reveals the lane.
- **Plaza:** driven by `p`.

For loudness, console standards sit at -24 LKFS with peaks no higher than -1 dB (dBTP). Portable targets are louder: Sony's is -18 and GANG's mobile target is -16 LUFS ([Designing Sound](https://designingsound.org/2013/02/loudness-in-game-audio/); [Audio Gang](https://www.audiogang.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/IESD-Mix-Ref-Levels-v03.02.pdf)). No web standard exists, so -16 to -18 LUFS is the reasonable target for a browser page. At -24, the page would sound quiet next to other web media.

Repetition is managed through selection logic rather than asset count alone. One production Wwise setup ran **15 variations in Shuffle mode with "avoid repeating last 10"** ([Audiokinetic Q&A](https://blog.audiokinetic.com/qa/3617/routing-puzzle-impossible-to-solve)). Two separate random containers can still repeat the same file back to back, so the fix is a shared parent ([Audiokinetic Q&A](https://blog.audiokinetic.com/qa/8949/how-can-i-avoid-repetition-from-different-containers?show=8960)). In JavaScript this becomes three rules:

- Keep a shuffle bag per pool, with an avoid-last count of about pool size minus one.
- Keep a shared history across surface changes, so stepping from asphalt onto a grate never repeats a file.
- Stack cheap multipliers: playback-rate and gain jitter, a few milliseconds of random start offset, step-timing jitter, an optional low-probability scuff layer, and slight variation in filter cutoff.

Together these multiply perceived variety from a small download. The Witcher 3 named "sound variations to avoid repetition" as a core ambience goal and used blend containers that react to parameters ([Audiokinetic](https://www.audiokinetic.com/en/learn/videos/vjuui_dw8cc/?course=wwise251&lesson=1)). The browser equivalent is two or three bed variants crossfaded slowly. Scatter categories need cooldowns and a global density cap. Very recognizable sounds, such as a pressure-cooker whistle from a window or a specific vendor call, need long cooldowns, because players notice them on the second hearing. No source directly measured listener fatigue from constant beds. The consistent practice is low, band-limited, slowly varying beds with room for silence.

## Thirty voices, two convolvers and Opus fit the browser budget

The two biggest costs in the browser are HRTF panning and decoded memory, not node count. Google's Raymond Toy warned that **the HRTF panner needs significant resources**, both to load its database and to process audio, which matters on low-end phones ([W3C public-audio, 2014](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-audio/2014JulSep/0033.html)). Each HRTF source costs two convolutions ([songbird-audio](https://www.npmjs.com/package/songbird-audio)). Equal-power panning is not free either: in one Firefox benchmark a single PannerNode accounted for about 20% of the time ([Mozilla Bugzilla 1851758](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1851758)). The spec default is equal-power, but three.js `PositionalAudio` reportedly sets HRTF in its constructor ([three.js docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/PositionalAudio.html)). Check `panner.panningModel` at runtime and set it per tier. Resonance Audio encodes all sources into one ambisonic field and applies HRTFs once, targeting "hundreds of simultaneous sources" ([Google blog](https://blog.google/products/google-ar-vr/resonance-audio-multi-platform-spatial-audio-scale)). For a single lane with tens of emitters, though, tiered PannerNodes are simpler, and Resonance appears little-maintained since about 2018 to 2019. That maintenance point is inferred from the projects' age, not verified.

Memory sets the format plan. A decoded buffer is 32-bit float, so **one minute of stereo at 48 kHz is about 23 MB in RAM against about 0.7 MB as 96 kb/s Opus**. Long beds should therefore stream through `<audio>` plus `MediaElementAudioSourceNode`, and only short one-shots should be decoded. Howler's `html5: true` streaming exists for the same reason ([howler](https://npmjs.com/package/howler)). WebM Opus works across iOS and macOS from Safari 17.4 ([WebKit thread](https://lists.webkit.org/pipermail/webkit-unassigned/2023-October/1126809.html)). However, `decodeAudioData` once failed even when `canPlayType` reported support ([WebKit Bug 245428](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=245428)). Ship an AAC fallback and wrap decoding in try/catch.

Lifecycle handling is not optional:

- Chrome starts an AudioContext `suspended` until a user gesture ([Chrome Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/web-audio-autoplay)). The click that starts the walk or engages pointer lock should create the context or resume it.
- iOS obeys the silent switch unless `navigator.audioSession.type = 'playback'` is set. This API is Safari-only ([WebKit Bug 237322](https://auto-bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=237322)).
- Suspend the context on `visibilitychange` to save battery.

| Resource | Budget for the gully (starting point) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Concurrent voices | About 24 to 32, with a pool and stop/disconnect beyond range | No sourced figure exists; inverse roll-off never silences sources on its own |
| HRTF panners | 4 to 8 nearest or important emitters; equal-power for the rest; beds and own footsteps unpanned | Two convolutions per HRTF source |
| Convolvers | 2 shared (lane, plaza), IRs of 1 to 1.5 s | No spec cap on IR length; truncated tails sound nearly the same; a 2 s stereo IR is about 0.77 MB |
| Filters | 1 to 2 biquads per live emitter, updated at 10 to 20 Hz with `setTargetAtTime` | Avoids zipper noise and per-frame work on the main thread |
| Occlusion rays | 5 to 10 Hz per emitter, staggered, against a collision proxy | Zone flags can replace rays in a single lane |
| Assets | Opus mono 48 to 64 kb/s for emitters, stereo 96 to 128 kb/s for beds; AAC fallback | Size on disk vs size decoded in RAM |
| AudioWorklet | Only for procedural wind or rain | `start(when)` is already sample-accurate |

The signal graph that delivers all of this is compact:

```
emitter:  BufferSource -> distanceLPF -> [send tap] -> occlusionLPF -> occlusionGain -> Panner -> categoryBus
send tap: -> sendGain -> laneConvolver  -> laneWet  (gain = cos(p*pi/2)) -> master
                      -> plazaConvolver -> plazaWet (gain = sin(p*pi/2)) -> master
beds:     MediaElementSource -> bedGain(p) -> bedBus
master:   categoryBuses -> DynamicsCompressor (limiter) -> destination
```

Some limits apply to everything above. Most of the AAA evidence came from search-result extracts of interviews and GDC decks, not full reads, and the Insomniac and Naughty Dog GDC talks that likely hold street-canyon and occlusion detail were not accessible. No public source gives voice counts, attenuation distances by sound category, or crossfade times for the titles cited. There is also no current benchmark for how many HRTF panners an iPhone sustains. Budgets should be confirmed with the render-capacity readout in Chrome's WebAudio DevTools and on a real iOS device, not trusted as fixed numbers.

## Conclusion

The research shifts where effort should go. Realistic spatialization matters less than authoring density and variation. GTA V's ratio of rules to static emitters, Sucker Punch's move to data-driven spawning and the Witcher's variation goals all point the same way: a small team gets the most life per hour from a rule-driven scatter layer with cooldowns, not from more hand-placed loops or brighter beds. The second insight is that a few well-chosen controls do most of the work. A single position-derived value `p` can drive the beds, the reverb blend and the stage reveal. A send taken before the occlusion filter makes sounds bend around corners. Stride-based triggering keeps footsteps, head bob and cadence consistent automatically.

The practical implication for the gully is to build the bus graph, the reverb send tap and `p` first, then spend the remaining budget on recorded content and variation. The two decisions most likely to go wrong without device testing are the number of HRTF panners and how memory is handled, so those should be measured early. Head bob is the one place where the evidence argues against the usual game default. Keep sprint bob amplitude nearly flat while frequency rises, and ship an off switch from the start.
